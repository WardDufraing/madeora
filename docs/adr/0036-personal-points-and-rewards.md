# ADR 0036: Personal points and rewards

- **Status:** Accepted for S28b within the approved architecture (§4, §6, §9, §15, §19, §21, §34, §36, §39, §43), CLAUDE.md (database access A, authorization C, realtime C, data fetching B, forms A, date/time A, €0 budget) and the spec's requirement that rewards and points exist in both the personal and the Home context (PS L2495–L2502). The owner decided the boundary and the self-defined reward model on 2026-10-28; the other defaults below are open to correction by the owner.
- **Date:** 2026-10-28
- **Step:** S28b (brief: `plans/steps/S28b-brief.md`). Builds on ADR 0030 (Home points and rewards), S07 (personal context) and ADR 0021 (dashboard engine). It changes nothing in ADR 0030.

## Context

S23 built a Home ledger: every S23 table has a `home_id`, and Home roles (`manageHomePoints`, `manageHomeRewards`, `approveHomeRewards`) decide who changes it. The spec also wants points and rewards in the Personal context, which belongs to the individual and must work with zero Homes (PS L55–L56, L91, L94–L100). The done-condition is that a person with zero Homes can hold a balance, define a reward and redeem it, and that no other account, Home role or staff path can read or change that ledger, while Home points stay unchanged.

## Decision

**A separate ledger with no Home in it.** `20261111090000_personal_points_rewards.sql` adds `private.personal_point_accounts`, `personal_point_entries`, `personal_rewards`, `personal_reward_redemptions` and `personal_reward_redemption_events`. None has a `home_id`, and no S23 table gains a nullable one, so no Home query can ever reach a personal row. Every table is keyed by `user_id` (references `auth.users`, on delete cascade); the child tables have composite keys `(user_id, id)`, so one account's ids never collide with another's and every lookup is by the caller's id plus the row id. The migration comments mark the rows as personally owned and in scope for the later personal export and account deletion.

**Only the owner, through definer functions.** RLS is enabled, nothing is granted on the tables, and the private helpers are revoked by name. Every read and change is a `security definer` function in `public` that takes the owner from `private.require_confirmed_caller()` (`auth.uid()`) and never from an argument. There is no staff function, no Home-role check that lets anyone through and no guardian path; another account, a Home Owner, an Administrator with every points capability and a staff account all get `not_found` or an empty answer, which does not reveal that a ledger exists. `packages/domain/src/points` adds `canUsePersonalLedger` (the owner's own account session only, never a Household Display) as the shared rule for later clients such as the Household Display (S43); today's web pages only ever use the owner's own session, and it decides nothing on the server.

**Ledger.** Append-only: a trigger refuses any update of an entry or a redemption event, and rows go only with their account (when the auth user is deleted). Entries and redemptions are numbered per account, never by a shared counter, so a page cursor reveals nothing about other accounts. Kinds: `credit` (positive, reason required), `correction` (either sign, reason required), `manual_reversal` (reverses one credit or correction, once, reason required), `redemption` (negative) and `refund` (positive, once per redemption). Each entry stores the amount, the balance before and after, `attributed_at`, the reason and its links; the actor is always the owner. The cached `balance` on the account row equals the sum of entries (checked by the tests). Nothing makes the balance negative: a redemption or negative correction beyond it answers `insufficient_points` and posts nothing. Client ids make a retry return `exists` with the same entry. Earned is credits, corrections and reversals; spent is redemptions less refunds. No points expire and nothing resets.

**Rewards (self-defined only, owner's decision).** The owner defines their own rewards: name 1–80, optional description up to 500, cost 1–100 000, enabled, optional availability window, optional total limit and optional per-period limit (week, month, year or ever), archived flag and revision; at most 200 non-archived rewards. Those numbers are S23's `POINTS_LIMITS`. An edit is previewed (times redeemed so far) and never changes past redemptions, which keep their cost and name. Archiving hides a reward from the catalog and the widget and keeps its history.

**Redemption is immediate.** There is no request, reservation or reviewer: a second person may not see the ledger, so the Home independent-reviewer rule cannot apply. The preview shows the cost and the balance after; confirming posts the `redemption` entry at once, refusing a changed cost (`cost-changed`), a disabled, archived, out-of-window or exhausted reward, or a reached period limit (with the next eligible date). Fulfillment is **Pending** → **Fulfilled** or **Unable to fulfill**, set by the owner; a change back needs a reason. Unable to fulfill is resolved once, by a **Refund** (one linked `refund` entry) or a **Replacement** (a short note of what the owner chose instead, never charged again). A resolved redemption is final. Every change takes `expected_revision`.

**Time.** Reward windows and period limits use the owner's own "today" and week start: the app passes the account's effective timezone date and regional week start (`personalClock`), and the database accepts a date only within a day of UTC's. A Home's timezone is never used.

**Locks.** Every change locks the owner's `personal_point_accounts` row first (`private.lock_personal_account`), then the reward, then the redemption. It never locks a Home `point_accounts` row in the same transaction. Two redemptions racing for the last points therefore spend once, and a doubled reversal or refund posts once.

**No notifications and no audit events (defaults, deviating from the brief's default 6).** Every change on this ledger is made by its only reader, and the codebase never notifies the actor of their own change, so a notice would only repeat the confirmation they just saw. Like personal habits, personal changes write no Home `audit_events`. The redemption events table keeps the fulfillment history the owner sees.

**Interface and API.** `/points` (available, earned and spent, the ledger with reversal, **Add points or correct**) and `/rewards` (the catalog with Redeem, new, edit with preview, archive and bring back, and the redemptions with fulfillment and resolution), in the Personal context with or without Homes; each links to the other. The sidebar gets no entry, as it has none for Home points. The personal dashboard gains **Personal points** (`personal-points`): the available balance and the cheapest reward the owner can afford; it is offered on the personal surface only, never on a Home dashboard or a Household Display. Routes are `GET /api/v1/personal-points`, `POST /api/v1/personal-point-adjustments`, `POST /api/v1/personal-point-entries/:id/reversal`, `GET/POST /api/v1/personal-rewards`, `PATCH /api/v1/personal-rewards/:id`, `GET/POST /api/v1/personal-reward-redemptions` and `PATCH /api/v1/personal-reward-redemptions/:id`, with the CSRF, JSON, size, session and outcome mapping of S23. Every body is a strict object, and a `homeId`, `userId` or `accountId` in the query or body is refused with 400. `GET /api/v1/points` and every Home page are unchanged. No Realtime: the pages refresh after the owner's own change.

## Consequences

- Any new write on this ledger must lock the account row first, or two changes could spend the same points.
- A future personal export (S38) and account deletion (S40) select these rows by `user_id`. The cascade on `auth.users` removes them when the auth user is deleted; if the deletion step removes the auth user at the start of the 30-day recovery rather than the end, it must keep these rows until final deletion instead.
- PS L658's export categories do not name points or rewards; the export step adds that category on purpose.
- Self-posted credits are the only way to earn personal points (brief default 1). No module awards them automatically; a later approved reward rule could add a kind.
- Guardian and child access (S41) must not assume a guardian can see this ledger; that question is open.
- The migration is forward-only like every other; repairing it on production means a new migration.
