# ADR 0042: Native calendar

- **Status:** Accepted for S32 within the approved architecture (§4, §6, §9, §15, §19, §21, §34, §36, §39, §43), CLAUDE.md (authorization C, database access A, forms A, internationalization A, date/time A, €0 budget), ADR 0011 and 0014 (permission engine, delegable capabilities), ADR 0020 (trash), ADR 0021 (dashboards) and ADR 0029 (Home vacations). The defaults in the brief (`plans/steps/S32-brief.md`, "Defaults picked") are open to correction by the owner.
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Step:** S32 (brief: `plans/steps/S32-brief.md`).

## Context

The spec (Modules.md §4.2 and the confirmed decisions it cites) gives every adult a personal calendar and every Home a shared one: native events with participants, recurring events edited for this event, this and following events or all events, personal events that stay private unless shared, and Home calendar permissions where editing and deleting are separate and the creator of an event has no extra rights. The done-condition for this step is that unshared events reveal nothing, not even busy time, in every surface, proven by an RLS test.

## Decision

**One table, two owners.** `private.calendar_events` holds personal events (an owner, no Home) and Home events (a Home; the creator is recorded but grants nothing). Title 1–200, optional location (≤200) and description (≤4000). An event is all-day (start date and inclusive end date, at most 366 days) or timed (local start and end plus an IANA timezone, at most 14 days). The timezone defaults to the context's (the Home's, or the owner's effective personal timezone) and is stored with the event, so an instant never moves when someone changes their own timezone. A local time in a daylight-saving gap is refused with `nonexistent_time` (the form says so and offers the next time that exists); a time in a fall-back overlap uses the second of the two, standard time, as PostgreSQL resolves it (the form says so). A recurring occurrence whose time falls in a gap moves forward by the gap, as PostgreSQL resolves it. No table is granted to an API role; every read and write is a security-definer function.

**Visibility.** `private.calendar_can_see(event, user, context)`: a personal event is visible to its owner in the Personal context, and to current members of a Home in that Home's context only when the owner shared it there (`private.calendar_event_shares`, keyed to the owner's membership, so leaving the Home removes the share). A Home event is visible to that Home's current members. Every read takes the context and returns only that context's events: the Personal calendar shows the person's own events, a Home calendar its events and the personal events shared with it, never a busy block, count or hint of anything else. An event that is not visible answers exactly like one that does not exist (`null` / `not_found`), also for a forged id, in every function, the widget, the trash and errors; audit events are written for Home events only, with ids.

**Permissions** (`packages/domain/src/permissions/calendar.ts`, the same rules as `private.calendar_level`). A personal event is its owner's alone: only they edit, delete and share it; shared, it is read-only for everyone else. In a Home, the Owner and Administrators with the new delegable capability `manageHomeCalendar` are calendar managers. Every other member has a level in `private.home_calendar_access`: _view_, _edit_ (the default, no row) or _edit and delete_. Managers set other members' levels, never their own or another manager's; changes are audited (`calendar.access.changed`) and effective on the next request; the row goes with the membership.

**Recurrence and scopes.** The S19 rules (daily, weekly on chosen weekdays, monthly with the last-day fallback, yearly with 29 February → 28 February; every 1–99; never, until a date or 1–999 times), evaluated on local dates in the event's timezone, so a timed series keeps its wall-clock time across daylight-saving changes. Occurrences are computed when read; `private.calendar_event_exceptions` (keyed by the original date) hold a changed or cancelled occurrence. _This event_ writes an exception (only the changed fields) or a cancellation. _This and following_ ends the series at its last occurrence before that date and starts a new series (client-chosen id, so a retry answers the same) with the changes; later exceptions move with it. _All events_ changes the series itself; exceptions whose date no longer fits the new rule are removed. Every scope has a dry run that lists the affected occurrences (and how many changed occurrences would be removed) before anything changes. Every change carries the revision and a stale one is refused.

**Trash.** Deleting a single event or a whole series is a 7-day trash entry (`calendar_event`, module `calendar`), visible and restorable only for its owner (personal) or for members whose level allows delete (Home); restoring needs room under the 5000-event limit and drops participants who have left. Deleting _this event_ or _this and following_ is not in the trash, and the dialog says so before it is confirmed.

**Vacations.** A Home calendar shows each confirmed, non-cancelled Home vacation as an all-day banner over its dates (overlapping ones as separate banners), with the vacation's own name or the generic label, linking to the vacation's page, which applies its own rules. `private.calendar_view_preferences` keeps each person's settings per calendar (the Personal one, or a Home's): "Show Home vacations" (a Home's, default on), "Show tasks" (any calendar, default off) and "Show chores" (a Home's, default off). Vacations and chores never appear on the Personal calendar.

**Task and chore overlays.** With Show tasks on, the calendar reads the context's task calendar (`list_task_calendar`, the S19c function, unfiltered: every status) and shows each task on its due date and, marked "Starts", on its start date, with Task, Done, Overdue, Planned and Repeats in words, linking to the task; tasks without a due date are counted with a link to them in Tasks. With Show chores on, a Home calendar reads `list_chore_schedule` (S21) and shows each chore occurrence with its state in the Chores words, linking to the occurrence (a date a recurring chore has not reached yet is not a link). Both are views of the same records with the viewer's session: nothing becomes an event, and turning an overlay off changes no task or chore. Their own filters stay in the Tasks and Chores calendars. A failed overlay read says so and leaves the events shown.

**Reminders.** An event may have one reminder for all its occurrences (`reminder_minutes`: at the start, 15 minutes, 1 hour or 1 day before; the pet-care set), set with the whole event and kept by a one-occurrence change. Its moment is the occurrence's start (08:00 on an all-day one's first day) in the event's timezone, minus the offset ("1 day before" keeps the wall-clock time). Triggers on events and exceptions queue the next occurrence's reminder in the outbox (`calendar-event-reminder`, payload: event id, rule date and moment); `deliver_calendar_reminder` (service role) rechecks the event, the occurrence and the moment, notifies in the `reminders` category with ids only, then queues the next one. A personal event reminds its owner only, whether shared or not; a Home event reminds its participants who are still members, or its creator when it has none. Someone's shared event never shows its reminder. The notice's title is read at display time with the reader's access (`calendar_notice_titles`), so a notice never names an event the reader may no longer see.

**Interface and API.** Calendar in the personal and Home navigation (`/calendar`, `/homes/[homeId]/calendar`) with month, week, day and agenda views kept in the address (no JavaScript needed), the person's week start, times in the context's timezone and the event's own when it differs, "Shared by {name}" on shared events, and the agenda list on small screens. Event pages, new and edit pages (React Hook Form with the shared Zod contract), a Calendar card on the member page and the `upcoming-events` widget (personal, Home and Home-sourced; next 3, 5 or 10 within 7, 14 or 30 days; added to the personal starter only). Routes: `GET /api/v1/calendar`, `POST /api/v1/calendar/events`, `GET/PATCH/DELETE /api/v1/calendar/events/:id`, `PUT /api/v1/calendar/events/:id/shares`, `GET/PUT /api/v1/homes/:homeId/calendar-access`, `PUT /api/v1/calendar/preferences`; restore goes through the shared trash.

**Limits.** 5000 events per person and per Home (a series counts once), 500 exceptions per series, 20 participants, 20 shared Homes; `private.calendar_limit` and `CALENDAR_LIMITS` in `apps/web/src/calendar/schema.ts`.

## Consequences

- Every later reader of events (Google Calendar in S33–S34, Ora, Household Displays, search, export) must read through these functions, so the same visibility rule applies and an unshared event stays invisible.
- Any new way a membership ends must keep removing that member's shares and calendar level (the foreign keys do this today).
- A reminder looks for the next occurrence among the following 1000 rule dates, in rule order; an occurrence moved before an earlier one is reminded after it is reached, and one moved more than 15 days later than its date is not found.
